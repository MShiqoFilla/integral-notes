$$\int_0^1 \sin^2(\log x)\ dx$$

Solution

Let $y = -\log x \Leftrightarrow x = e^{-y} \Rightarrow dx = -e^{-y}\ dy$. Therefore \begin{align*} A &= \int_{0}^{1}\sin^2(\log x)\ dx = -\int_{\infty}^{0} \sin^2y \cdot e^{-y}\ dy = \int_{0}^{\infty} \left(\dfrac{1-\cos 2y}{2}\right)e^{-y}\ dy \\ &= \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\ dy - \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\cos 2y\ dy = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\cos 2y\ dy. \end{align*} We focus on the last (rightmost) integral by implementing integration by parts, set $u=e^{-y} \Rightarrow du = -e^{-y}\ dy$, and $dv=\cos 2y\ dy \Rightarrow v = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2y$, \begin{align*} B=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\cos 2y\ dy = \left[\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-y}\sin 2y\right]^{\infty}_0+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\sin 2y\ dy = \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\sin 2y\ dy. \end{align*} Now we have to do another integration by parts, set $u=e^{-y} \Rightarrow du = -e^{-y}\ dy$, and $dv=\sin 2y\ dy \Rightarrow v = -\dfrac{1}{2}\cos 2y$ gives us \begin{align*} B=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\sin 2y\ dy = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\left[-\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-y}\cos 2y\right]^{\infty}_0-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\cos 2y\ dy\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{2} -\dfrac{1}{2}B\right) = \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{4}B. \end{align*} Therefore $B=\dfrac{1}{5}$. Hence we get the solution for $A$, \begin{equation*} A = \dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}B = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}. \end{equation*}
