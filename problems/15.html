$$\int\dfrac{\cos x+x\sin x}{x(x+\cos x)}\ dx$$

Solution

First we need to notice that \begin{equation*} \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x(x+\cos x)\right) = 2x+\cos x-x\sin x. \end{equation*} Now doing some algebraic manipulation on the numerator we get \begin{align*} \int\dfrac{\cos x+x\sin x}{x\left(x+\cos x\right)}\ dx &= \int \dfrac{2x+\cos x-x\sin x}{x^2+x\cos x}\ dx - \int \dfrac{2x(1-\sin x)}{x(x + \cos x)}\ dx \\ &=\int \dfrac{d(x^2+x\cos x)}{x^2+x\cos x} - 2\int \dfrac{1-\sin x}{x+\cos x}\ dx \\ &= \log \left(x^2+x\cos x\right) - 2\int\dfrac{d(x+\cos x)}{x+\cos x} \\ &= \log \left(x^2+x\cos x\right) - 2\log(x+\cos x) + C \\ &= \log\left[\dfrac{x^2+x\cos x}{(x+\cos x)^2}\right] + C = \log\left(\dfrac{x}{x+\cos x}\right) + C. \end{align*}
